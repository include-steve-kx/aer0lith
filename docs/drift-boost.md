# Drift and boost

Aer0lith measures drift as the angle between the aircraft's nose and its controllable velocity. Explosion force and scripted dodge displacement are deliberately excluded, so only deliberate flight can earn energy.

Hold `J` or the large touch drift control to reduce lateral grip. Turning rotates the aircraft while momentum continues along its prior path. Drift energy charges from slip angle, controllable speed, and fixed simulation time. Releasing drift banks the energy briefly before passive decay begins.

Hold `K` or the large touch boost control to use the bank. The current energy automatically selects tier I, II, or III; the player never selects a tier manually. Energy drains more quickly while powered, and the boost live-downgrades as thresholds are crossed. The first boost hold for a newly earned bank adds one nose-biased ignition kick. Releasing and reholding cannot repeat that kick. When the bank reaches zero, a continuing hold becomes the weaker normal boost.

Pressing drift during boost cancels it. An active drift-boost loses its remaining bank and immediately begins a fresh drift. If boost is still held, it remains suppressed until released and pressed again.

The chase cameras favor travel direction during drift and return elastically toward the nose afterward. The player-tuned default is deliberately tight: 0.07 s position response, 0.1 s heading/recovery/bank response, 80% travel influence, and a 24° lag cap. Cockpit view remains rigid. The central slip cue and three-segment annular meter expose the same authoritative state used by physics, camera, wind, wake, and motion blur. The meter's circle is centered on the screen with a 25vw outer radius, placing its right edge at 75% screen width; sharp radial cuts divide its outlined sector into equal tiers.

Drifting emits a separate thick ash trail from one centerline anchor behind the rear fuselage. It is a single fixed-capacity particle draw call and only creates particles while drift is actively held above the scoring angle; ordinary cruise and normal boost do not emit it. The tuned defaults are 64 particles/s, 10 m cloud diameter, 2 s life, 55% opacity, and 6 m/s turbulence. Rate, size, life, opacity, turbulence, and color are live settings. The older thin wing lines remain boost feedback.

Terrain and meteor contact no longer enters an error/recovery state. The solver works in surface-relative velocity, applies low restitution to incoming normal speed, removes tangential speed with configurable surface friction, and adds a small separation speed only on the first contact step. Moving meteors contribute their surface velocity. Explosion velocity is filtered through the same contact plane before controllable velocity is derived, so the combined result exactly matches the response. Dissipated energy drives a bounded, one-draw pool of world-space sparks. The tuned preset uses 0.6 friction and larger white sparks: 3× amount, 0.15 m thickness, 4 m streak length, 50 m/s launch speed, 0.8 s life, and 1× spread. The closest rendered aircraft triangle and coincident vertices flash red without adding an impact sphere. Flight mode, drift bank, boost, combat state, and player control continue uninterrupted; only an active dodge roll is canceled.

The screen-fixed route arrow targets the procedural route center ahead of the aircraft and remains present in both Analysis and Ambient modes. It is a small, thin white open-headed 3D model implemented with lightweight DOM geometry and one CSS 3D orientation rather than a Three.js world or camera object. When the route aligns with the camera, the arrow points into the screen; its shaft becomes visible sideways or vertically as the upcoming route diverges. Settings independently control body length, head length, head width, line thickness, and whether the quadrangular head is four open ribs or four solid triangular faces. Reverse world-Z progress must persist before the smaller red `WRONG WAY` label appears, and forward progress clears it separately. Route-generation controls scale horizontal/vertical deviation and clearance; they remain pending until **Apply & Restart Current World** or **New World**.

All handling, impact, energy, tier, camera, display, trail, navigation, and input parameters are under System Settings. Sections start collapsed and remember which groups the player unfolds. Hovering any section or setting name shows a short explanation. Live-safe values update immediately. Settings persist under `aer0lith.settings.v4`; section layout uses its own `aer0lith.settings-sections.v1` key. Existing v3 and `aer0lith.visual-settings.v2` values migrate automatically, while obsolete collision-push fields are ignored. The drift meter is a three-part annular sector on a circle centered at the reticle with an outer radius of 25vw; centerline arc length and radial thickness are independently adjustable.

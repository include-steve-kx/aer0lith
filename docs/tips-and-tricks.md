# Tips and tricks

Aer0lith rewards two different styles. You can fly safely and let the systems clear danger for you, or fly close to danger and turn sideways momentum into drift energy. The best runs switch between both styles.

## Read the two directions

The center crosshair shows where the nose points. The nearby slip cue shows where the aircraft is actually moving.

When those directions separate, the aircraft is slipping. More sideways speed means more tail ash and faster energy charging. A useful habit is to watch the cue instead of only watching the nose: the nose tells you where you are steering next, while the cue tells you where you will go right now.

## Turn before the opening, not inside it

A hard turn temporarily reduces grip, so the aircraft keeps some of its old momentum while the nose rotates. Start turning just before a wide bend or chamber. The aircraft can slide toward the opening while already pointing toward the next section.

This creates energy without requiring a special Drift button. In a narrow tunnel, use smaller early turns. A last-second hard turn creates a bigger slide but leaves less room to recover.

## Bank energy, then spend it on the exit

You do not need to boost as soon as energy appears. Build energy during a turn, straighten the nose toward a safe opening, and then press Boost. This converts the stored bank into the current drift-boost tier when the extra speed is useful.

Unused energy waits briefly and then drains, so do not save it forever. The meter color shows the current tier. While it is charging or powering drift boost, its pulse makes the active state easier to recognize without reading the label.

## Upgrade a normal boost without giving up speed

If you start normal Boost with no energy and then earn energy while still holding it, drift boost does not activate automatically. Release Boost and quickly press it again. The short re-press window keeps the normal thrust alive while switching into the earned tier.

This is especially useful after an explosion pushes you sideways during a normal boost.

## Safe route: scan and let missiles work

Scanning reveals meteors. Detected meteors in front of the aircraft can be reserved and attacked by the auto-tracking missiles when they are close enough and have a clear line of sight.

Use this style in tight terrain:

1. Scan before entering the constriction.
2. Stay near the route center and let missiles remove exposed meteors.
3. Wait for the explosions to finish before taking the narrowest line.

This is slower but predictable. The tradeoff is that distant explosions usually give little or no useful push, so they generate less explosion-driven slip energy.

## Risk route: use a meteor like a banked turn

A nearby meteor explosion pushes the aircraft away from the meteor. That push contributes to automatic slip energy.

Imagine a corridor with most meteors near its bottom-left side. Fly near them but keep your aircraft on their top-right side. When one explodes, the push is generally toward the top-right: away from the meteors and toward the open part of the corridor. You gain sideways velocity and energy without asking the explosion to push you through the wall.

The proximity fuse has a short randomized delay after it arms, so never aim at one exact escape point. Leave a safe region where any likely detonation time still works.

## Use explosions as an emergency turn

An explosion can act like a course-changing shove. If terrain blocks the middle and a meteor is also ahead near the middle, pass the meteor on the side opposite your desired escape direction. The blast then pushes you sideways around the terrain, similar to a dodge that also creates eligible slip.

For example, pass just left of the meteor when the safe opening is farther left. Because the aircraft is left of the meteor at detonation, the outward push continues left and helps clear the obstacle.

This move works best when you have already pointed the nose toward the escape route. The explosion changes the travel direction; your nose decides how quickly normal thrust turns that shove into controlled forward motion.

## Scan the arrows, but control the weapon

Scanned meteors show predicted push arrows. These use the same impulse calculation as the real explosion, so they are useful for planning.

If you want the information without an immediate missile launch, disable missiles in Settings before scanning. You can then choose when to destroy a meteor with bullets or the Pulse Cannon. This turns an unpredictable proximity blast into a more deliberate maneuver.

Bullets need several hits on larger meteors, which gives fine timing control. The Pulse Cannon can clear several meteors in one line, but multiple simultaneous pushes add together. Meteors on opposite sides may cancel each other; meteors on the same side can create a much larger combined shove.

## Do not try to farm walls

Terrain contact can visibly throw the travel direction sideways, but collision-made lateral motion is temporarily excluded from energy charging. Scraping also removes speed through friction.

Use contact recovery to stay alive, not as an energy source. Clean turns and explosions preserve more speed and earn energy more reliably.

## Know what a dodge can and cannot do

The roll buttons are excellent for quickly changing position, and collision remains active during a roll. The scripted dodge displacement does not earn drift energy by itself.

Use a roll to avoid a wall or escape the dangerous side of an explosion. Use steering or explosion momentum when the goal is to charge the energy bank.

## Let pushes add instead of cancel

When several meteors explode close together, their pushes combine as vectors. A cluster on one side of the aircraft can create a strong useful shove. Explosions spread around both sides often cancel and still produce camera shake and danger.

Before firing into a group, ask one simple question: after adding all the arrows, which way will the aircraft move?

## Use autopilot as a setup tool

Autopilot keeps following the generated route, can earn slip energy on its turns, and stays engaged through boost and explosion pushes. It is useful for scanning a difficult section and building a small bank while you study the terrain.

Take manual control when you see a better line, then spend the bank on the exit. Switching between autopilot and manual flight is often faster than treating either one as the only way to play.

## A simple fast-run rhythm

Try this loop:

1. Look ahead with the route arrow and scan when the next section is hard to read.
2. Begin the next turn early and watch the slip cue move away from the crosshair.
3. Let the energy reach a useful tier while keeping an open recovery path.
4. Point the nose through the exit and boost.
5. If a well-positioned meteor is nearby, use its outward push to extend the slide or tighten the turn.
6. Straighten before the next narrow section, then repeat.

The important idea is not “always drift” or “always boost.” Fast flight comes from creating sideways momentum where there is room, then converting it back into forward progress before the terrain becomes tight.
